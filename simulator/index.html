Geometry and build

Every visible part is a rigid body from the official Project Hail Mary Rocky action-figure kit: torso, five upper limbs (A), five lower limbs (B) and the alternate open hand 1-C. Ball studs, pins and holes were located by RANSAC sphere and cylinder fits, and each part was registered to the supplied sculpture, so the joints sit where the kit puts them.

Each limb has three servos. Shoulder yaw and pitch pass through the ball centre, and the elbow turns on the kit's hinge axis, including its measured skew. The ball's third rotation is not needed to place a foot, so it is fixed.

Mass comes from exact surface and volume integrals of each mesh: an FDM PLA shell (2.4 mm walls, 15 % infill) plus 15 servos, a 2S battery and electronics. At the desk scale of 0.0045 m per print-mm the carapace is 18.2 cm across and Rocky weighs 2.73 kg. The Canon mass case scales to the book's 18 inches and 168 kg.

Rigid-body dynamics and contact

M(q) v̇ + c(q, v) = τ + Jᵀλ        CRBA / RNEA (Featherstone 2008)
(M + hD) v⁺ = M v + h(τ − c) + Jᵀλ  semi-implicit, h = 1 ms
q⁺ = q ⊕ h v⁺                       exact quaternion exponential
F = k δ^{3/2} (1 + α δ̇)             Hunt–Crossley pad, k = (4/3)E*√R
0 ≤ λₙ ⊥ vₙ⁺ + γλₙ − b ≥ 0          implicit, linearised about δ
‖λₜ‖ ≤ μ λₙ                         exact Coulomb disk (max. dissipation)

Constraint impulses come from projected Gauss–Seidel on the Delassus operator W = J(M + hD)⁻¹Jᵀ, warm-started and stopped at 10⁻⁷ N·s. Feet are TPU 95A spheres (PLA on the slippery floor); limb shells and the carapace carry collision spheres that also collide with each other. Blocks and friction patches make up the terrain. Nine analytic tests check the engine: free fall, Hertz penetration, the stick/slide boundary on an incline, pendulum periods, first-order energy and momentum convergence, and servo stall and no-load speed.

Servo constants are the published BAM identification of the Feetech STS3215 at 7.4 V (Duclusaud & Passault, ICRA 2025). Friction is a box constraint in the solver, so the gear train holds loads by stiction and keeps doing so when a servo loses power. The thermal network is calibrated to a bench test of the STS3215 (48 °C after a 10-minute 15 kg·cm hold). The firmware is modelled as on the Feetech memory table: 80 % duty for 2 s limits output to 20 % until a new goal, and 70 °C on the board sensor releases torque.

Control and stability

Δθ = Jᵀ (J Jᵀ + λ² I)⁻¹ e          damped least squares, true axes
p_td = home ⊕ pose(t_mid-stance)    Raibert foothold, IK-checked
δz_i += k (F_i − F_i*) dt           pad-force load balancing
v·T(1 − φ/n) ≤ S_max                stride (per-leg yaw room)
SSM = min_edges dist(COM⊥, edge)    McGhee & Frank
ξ = c + ċ / √(g / z)                capture point

The controller only reads what a robot could sense: quantised encoders, a noisy IMU, force sensors in the foot pads, servo error bits and a body-mounted depth sensor for the floor. Swing feet lift, travel and descend on a path shaped for the servo's lag, then approach the ground slowly until the pad fires. Stance loads are balanced with the pad forces, and footholds stay clear of block edges and joint limits. Stability metrics use measured contact forces, not the planned stance.

Known limits

  • Rocky's biological anatomy is not public, so the kinematics are a buildable robot interpretation of the kit, not the film rig.
  • Battery parameters and the servo winding thermal node are estimates; the 12 V STS3215, STS3250 and XM540 presets are datasheet-derived, not identified.
  • Collision shapes are spheres (feet, limb shells, carapace) against planes, blocks and each other, not the exact meshes.
  • Walking is a statically stable wave gait at 1–2 cm/s: the speed a 1:345 hobby servo can deliver on this build, not the film's scuttle.

Everything runs locally. The physics lives in a Web Worker, so rendering never waits on the solver.

Commands go through the gait planner, servo firmware and physics like any other scenario, so the robot answers with its real limits: it takes a second to start and stop, steps around joint limits, and actions wait until all five feet are down.
